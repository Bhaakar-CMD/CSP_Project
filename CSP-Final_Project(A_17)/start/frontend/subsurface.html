🌱 Subsurface Irrigation System

A modern method where water is applied directly to the root zone beneath the soil surface.

📖 Introduction

Subsurface irrigation is an advanced technique where water is delivered below the soil surface, directly to the plant root zone. It minimizes evaporation losses and improves water-use efficiency. This method is especially suitable for arid regions and high-value crops that require precise water management.

🔍 Types of Subsurface Irrigation

  • Natural Subsurface Irrigation – Utilizes natural underground water flow to moisten the root zone.
  • Artificial Subsurface Irrigation – Involves buried pipes or tubes that deliver water directly below the soil surface.

⚙️ Components of a Subsurface Irrigation System

  • Water Source – Well, borewell, or reservoir.
  • Pumping System – Pumps water into the subsurface pipes.
  • Distribution Pipes – Network of buried pipes or tubes beneath the soil.
  • Emitters or Perforations – Allow water to seep into the root zone.
  • Control Valves – Regulate pressure and flow of water.

🔧 How to Set Up

  1. Prepare the field and decide the layout for buried pipes.
  2. Install main pipelines connected to the water source.
  3. Lay lateral pipes 20–50 cm below the soil surface.
  4. Connect emitters or perforated sections to ensure even distribution.
  5. Test the system for leaks and adjust pressure as required.

▶️ How to Use

Turn on the pump to deliver water into the subsurface distribution pipes. Water will gradually seep into the root zone of crops through perforations or emitters. Monitor soil moisture levels to avoid over-irrigation.

🛠️ Maintenance Tips

  • Flush pipes regularly to prevent clogging from soil particles and salts.
  • Check emitters and perforations for blockages.
  • Maintain proper water pressure to ensure even distribution.
  • Inspect pipelines periodically for leaks or damage.
  • Avoid using poor-quality water that may cause sediment buildup.
