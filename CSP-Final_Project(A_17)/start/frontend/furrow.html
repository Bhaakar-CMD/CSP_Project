🌾 Furrow Irrigation System

A traditional surface irrigation method using small channels to distribute water between crop rows.

📖 Introduction

Furrow irrigation is a surface irrigation method where water flows through small parallel channels (called furrows) dug between crop rows. This technique allows controlled water flow and uniform distribution across the field. It is suitable for row crops such as maize, sugarcane, cotton, and vegetables.

🌿 Advantages of Furrow Irrigation

  • Requires less water compared to flood irrigation.
  • Reduces water loss due to evaporation and deep percolation.
  • Suitable for sloped lands with moderate infiltration rates.
  • Facilitates field operations such as weeding and harvesting.
  • Minimizes plant stem and leaf wetting, reducing disease risk.

⚙️ Components of a Furrow Irrigation System

  • Water Source: Well, canal, or reservoir supplying irrigation water.
  • Main Channel: Conveys water to the field.
  • Field Channels: Distribute water to different furrow sets.
  • Furrows: Small parallel ditches between crop rows for water flow.
  • Control Gates or Checks: Regulate flow to avoid runoff and erosion.

🔧 How to Set Up

  1. Level the field slightly to ensure gentle slope (0.2–0.5%).
  2. Form parallel furrows 30–90 cm apart depending on crop type.
  3. Construct a main channel and sub-channels for uniform water supply.
  4. Install small control gates or earthen checks to regulate water entry.
  5. Ensure water reaches the end of each furrow without runoff.

▶️ How to Use

Water is released into the furrows from the head channel and flows slowly between the crop rows. Once the required amount of water infiltrates into the root zone, the flow is stopped or shifted to the next set of furrows. Proper timing ensures efficient water use without waterlogging.

🛠️ Maintenance Tips

  • Repair eroded furrows and bunds regularly.
  • Clean channels to prevent blockage by sediment or weeds.
  • Monitor slope and infiltration rates to maintain even distribution.
  • Avoid excessive water flow to prevent soil erosion.
  • Reform furrows each season to maintain shape and efficiency.
